00 / NATIONAL AGRICULTURE SNAPSHOT

Nigeria’s agricultural landscape

Nigeria’s agricultural strengths are spread across six zones, each shaped by climate, land resources, market access, and local infrastructure. This snapshot helps position the Hub within the national farming map and identifies the highest-potential crop and livestock corridors across the country.

Primary production clusters

North Central & North West

Grains, root crops, cereals and livestock remain central to national output, with Benue, Kebbi, Kano and Kaduna standing out.

Major constraints

Insecurity, roads & storage

Banditry, poor rural transport, flooding and post-harvest losses continue to weaken both production and market efficiency.

Top commodity zones

Cocoa, rice, yam & fisheries

Ondo and Cross River anchor cocoa; Kebbi and Ebonyi lead rice; Benue and Taraba remain strong in yam and cassava.

Key trading hubs

Jos, Kano, Lagos & Port Harcourt

From Dawanau to Mile 12 and Port Harcourt, urban markets remain critical funnels for food aggregation and trade.

North Central

  • Benue — Yam, cassava, rice, sorghum, soybean and beniseed; strong aggregation markets in Makurdi, Otukpo and Gboko.
  • Kogi — Cassava, yam, rice, maize, cashew and oil palm; Lokoja, Kabba and Okene are key trading hubs.
  • Kwara — Maize, cassava, yam, rice, sorghum, tomatoes and poultry; Ilorin, Offa and Patigi are active commercial markets.
  • Nasarawa — Yam, cassava, rice, maize, sesame and ginger; Lafia, Keffi and Akwanga are important trade points.
  • Niger — Rice, maize, sorghum, yam, cassava, shea, ginger and large livestock production; major hubs in Minna, Bida and Suleja.
  • Plateau — Irish potato, tomatoes, cabbage, carrots, maize and dairy; Jos and Bokkos lead in potato and vegetable trade.
  • FCT, Abuja — Yam, cassava, maize, sorghum, millet, vegetables and peri-urban poultry and piggery.

North East

  • Adamawa — Maize, rice, cotton, groundnut, ginger and cattle; Mubi and Yola are major market points.
  • Bauchi — Sorghum, millet, cowpea, groundnut, cotton, maize and large cattle and goat populations.
  • Borno — Millet, sorghum, cowpea, groundnut, gum arabic, fishing and irrigated wheat; Maiduguri and Baga are key trading centres.
  • Gombe — Maize, sorghum, groundnut, cowpea, cotton and tomatoes; markets in Gombe and Dukku.
  • Taraba — Maize, rice, sorghum, yam, cassava, ginger and tea; Jalingo, Wukari and Bali are important nodes.
  • Yobe — Gum arabic, millet, sorghum, cowpea, groundnut, sesame and livestock; Potiskum is a major livestock hub.

North West

  • Jigawa — Rice, wheat, sorghum, sesame, millet and onions; irrigation and wetland farming drive output.
  • Kaduna — Maize, sorghum, ginger, cotton, soybean and tomatoes; Kaduna, Zaria and Kachia are strategic markets.
  • Kano — Millet, sorghum, groundnut, cowpea, onion, tomatoes, wheat and rice; Dawanau is one of West Africa’s largest grain markets.
  • Katsina — Millet, sorghum, maize, cowpea, groundnut, cotton and sesame; Funtua is an important grain market.
  • Kebbi — Rice, maize, sorghum, millet, wheat and onions; Birnin Kebbi and Argungu are key rice and fishing hubs.
  • Sokoto — Millet, sorghum, rice, cowpea, onions, cotton and goats; Sokoto central and Illela are important trade routes.
  • Zamfara — Millet, sorghum, groundnut, cowpea, rice and livestock; Gusau and Kaura Namoda are major trade centres.

South West

  • Ekiti — Cocoa, cassava, yam, oil palm, kolanut, citrus and plantain; strong market links via Ado-Ekiti and Ikere.
  • Lagos — Poultry, fisheries, aquaculture, vegetables and food processing; Mile 12 and Epe remain key perishables hubs.
  • Ogun — Cassava, maize, cocoa, kolanut, oil palm, rubber and poultry; Abeokuta, Ijebu-Ode and Sagamu are major trade routes.
  • Ondo — Cocoa, oil palm, cassava, yam, rubber, citrus and fishing; Akure, Ondo town and Ore are support centres.
  • Osun — Cocoa, cassava, yam, maize and oil palm; Oshogbo and Ilesa are trading hubs.
  • Oyo — Maize, cassava, yam, cocoa, cashew, plantain and large poultry and cattle sectors; Bodija and Ogbomoso are key market anchors.

South East

  • Abia — Cassava, yam, oil palm, maize, fruits, rubber and poultry; Aba and Umuahia are strong food commerce centres.
  • Anambra — Cassava, yam, rice, maize, oil palm and vegetables; Onitsha remains a major trade hub.
  • Ebonyi — Rice, yam, cassava, maize and cocoyam; Abakaliki is nationally known for rice production and milling.
  • Enugu — Cassava, yam, rice, maize, cocoyam, oil palm and poultry; Ogbete and Nsukka are notable trade points.
  • Imo — Cassava, yam, oil palm, maize, cocoyam, citrus and poultry; Eke Ukwu and Relief Market are important civic markets.

South South

  • Akwa Ibom — Oil palm, cassava, plantain, fishing and aquaculture; Uyo and Eket are important trade centres.
  • Bayelsa — Fishing, aquaculture, cassava, plantain, coconut and oil palm; Yenagoa and Brass are key fish and agro hubs.
  • Cross River — Cocoa, oil palm, rubber, cassava, yam and timber; Calabar and Ikom anchor trade and value chains.
  • Delta — Cassava, oil palm, rubber, yam, plantain and aquaculture; Warri, Asaba and Sapele are major nodes.
  • Edo — Rubber, oil palm, cassava, yam, cocoa, timber and pineapple; Benin City remains a major market and logistics centre.
  • Rivers — Fishing, aquaculture, cassava, oil palm, yam and swamp rice; Port Harcourt and Bonny are key logistics and fish hubs.

Quick pointers: Niger and Borno are among the largest land areas; Lagos, Anambra, Ebonyi and Imo are among the smallest; Ondo, Ekiti, Osun, Cross River and Ogun anchor the cocoa belt; Kebbi, Ebonyi, Niger, Kano and Benue are among the leading rice states; livestock hubs include Sokoto, Kebbi, Borno, Yobe and Bauchi; and fisheries are strongest in Bayelsa, Rivers, Delta, Akwa Ibom and Lagos.

01 / PRODUCE & MARKET ACCESS

Promote agricultural products and market access

State Produce Showcase

A recurring exhibition and trade fair rotating across states, where farmers and cooperatives present crops, livestock and processed goods to buyers, retailers and exporters.

Agro Trade Hub Africa Marketplace Days

Scheduled market-linkage events connecting producers directly with bulk buyers, food processors, schools, hospitals, hotels and other institutional purchasers.

“Made by Our Farmers” Branding Initiative

A shared label for produce sold through the Hub's network, helping build buyer trust and recognition for participating farmers.

Digital Produce Catalogue

A seasonal online listing where cooperatives share available produce so buyers across Nigeria can discover and place orders.

Cross-State Value Chain Tours

Site visits that connect producers in one state with processors and exporters in another, helping develop multi-state supply relationships.

Export Readiness Program

Support for larger cooperatives to meet the packaging, quality and documentation standards required for regional and international markets.

Agro Trade Hub Africa Trade Missions

Organized delegations of farmers and cooperative leaders to trade fairs and buyer events beyond their home state, opening new market relationships.

02 / FARMER VOICE & POLICY

Advocate for better agricultural policies

Farmer Policy Dialogues

Town-hall meetings where farmers raise concerns such as land access, input costs and transport, informing position papers for state and federal ministries.

Agro Trade Hub Africa Policy Brief Series

Regular, concise publications translating farmers' concerns into practical recommendations for legislators, ministries and the press.

State-Level Farmer Advocacy Councils

Representative groups in state networks that liaise with local government on fertilizer access, storage infrastructure, rural roads and related priorities.

Coalition Building with Cooperatives Associations

Joint advocacy with farmer unions and cooperative federations to amplify a unified voice on agricultural policy.

Annual State of Agriculture Report

A yearly publication bringing together state-network data and farmer testimony to track policy progress and promote institutional accountability.

Media and Press Engagement Program

Relationships with agricultural journalists and local media that keep farmer priorities visible in public discussion.

03 / YOUNG FARMERS & ENTERPRISE

Bring more young people into farming

Young Farmers Fellowship

A structured entry into farming that pairs interested young people with experienced farmers for mentorship in production and farm business.

Agri-Business Bootcamps for Youth

Practical short courses on launching a farm enterprise, accessing finance and connecting to the Hub's market network.

School and Campus Outreach

Talks and demonstration visits at secondary schools and universities presenting farming as a modern, viable business path.

Youth Farm Starter Grants and Micro-loans

Work with Verneldos and Ntonarche to develop small-scale finance or input-support packages for first-time young farmers.

Agri-Tech Youth Challenge

A competition inviting young people to apply technology, practical tools and social media marketing to farming operations across the Hub network.

Young Farmer Ambassadors

Fellowship graduates serve as peer mentors and local recruiters, sharing farming opportunities through their own communities.

Land Access Support for Youth

Work with local authorities and community leaders to help young people without inherited land find affordable land to farm.

04 / ORGANIC INPUTS & SOIL

Build awareness of organic fertilizers

Organic Fertilizer Demonstration Plots

Side-by-side farm plots demonstrate the effects of organic and synthetic inputs on yields and soil health.

Community Fertilizer Workshops

Hands-on learning in making and applying compost and organic fertilizer locally, helping reduce reliance on costly imported inputs.

“Soil Health First” Campaign

Radio, community meetings and printed materials share the long-term benefits of organic fertilizer for soil and produce quality.

Organic Input Cooperative Buying

Help farmer groups pool resources to buy or produce organic fertilizer at a lower cost.

Organic Fertilizer Production Training

Training for interested cooperatives to produce organic fertilizer at small commercial scale, creating income while supplying local farmers.

Soil Testing Days

Community events offering basic soil testing so farmers can understand their land's needs and make informed input choices.

05 / LOCAL FOOD & CONSUMER DEMAND

Encourage people to buy local and eat local

“Eat Nigerian, Grow Nigerian” Campaign

Public messaging encouraging households, restaurants and institutions to choose locally grown produce.

Local Produce Partnerships with Schools and Hospitals

Build relationships with institutional kitchens to source food directly from farmers in the Hub network.

Farmers' Markets in Urban Centers

Regular city markets bring local produce directly to urban consumers and shorten the supply chain.

Consumer Awareness Drives

Share accessible information on the nutritional and economic benefits of buying domestically grown food.

Restaurant and Hospitality Sourcing Partnerships

Direct supply agreements with restaurants and hotels interested in offering locally sourced menus.

Local Produce Cooking and Recipe Campaigns

Community and social content featuring ways to prepare popular local staples and build everyday demand.

06 / BLOCKCHAIN & DIGITAL AGRICULTURE

Build blockchain awareness and promote agricultural investment

Blockchain Farming Awareness Workshops

Community sessions explain how digital ledgers can support traceable supply chains, transparent payments and verifiable produce records.

Digital Traceability Pilot

A small cooperative pilot records harvest, transport and sale data so buyers can verify produce origin and quality.

“Farm Ledger” Demonstration

Hands-on demonstrations of digital record-keeping for proof of land use and sale, and support in resolving disputes.

Blockchain Investment Roundtables

Bring together agri-tech investors, fintech firms and development partners to explore funding useful digital tools for Nigerian farmers.

Youth Digital Farming Track

Pair the Young Farmers Fellowship with introductory blockchain and digital-skills training, positioning youth as digital agriculture leaders.

Partnership Scouting with African Agri-Tech Platforms

Identify existing African blockchain agriculture initiatives and explore pilot partnerships and knowledge exchange.

Farmer Digital Readiness Assessment

Survey phone and internet access and digital literacy across state networks before introducing tools that depend on digital access.

Digital Cooperative Wallets Pilot

Test simple blockchain-based payment wallets with one or two cooperatives so members can receive transparent payments directly.

Blockchain Literacy Champions

Train farmers in each state network to explain digital tools and provide practical support within their communities.

Join farmers, cooperatives and partners working to strengthen agriculture across Nigeria.
